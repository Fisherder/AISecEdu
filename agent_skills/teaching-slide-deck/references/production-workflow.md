# Production slide workflow

## Deck-level story map

Before page generation, write a table with page number, teaching purpose, source evidence, learner action, visual encoding, and transition. A page stays only if it advances the learning path. Use one visual idea per page and reuse a restrained motif rather than repeating a card grid.

Match visual form to meaning:

- sequence or causality: process, timeline, or annotated flow;
- differences and decisions: comparison with aligned criteria;
- system relationships: topology, concept map, or layered architecture;
- evidence: logs, code, packet fields, metrics, or before/after states;
- practice: task, available evidence, learner action, and completion standard;
- summary: a compact model, retrieval question, and transfer action.

## Visual system

Choose one typography scale, spacing rhythm, palette, and recurring motif for the deck. Preserve authored light or dark slide colors independent of the application theme. Use strong hierarchy and enough empty space. Avoid tiny text, decorative widgets that do not teach, repeated dashboards, and dense prose.

For each normal deck include at least one diagrammatic relationship, one evidence-rich case, one worked example or code/configuration walkthrough, and two learner interactions. Vary composition as well as labels.

## Quality passes

1. Content pass: facts, source coverage, objectives, examples, questions, and answers are accurate.
2. Narrative pass: every page has a purpose, transition, and no duplicated title or filler.
3. Visual pass: render every page and inspect overflow, overlap, clipping, contrast, density, and layout repetition.
4. Teaching pass: rehearse with the speaker scripts; confirm timing, questions, expected responses, misconception repair, and transfer.
5. Delivery pass: verify page count, download or preview behavior, and script synchronization after next/previous navigation.

Any failed pass triggers repair and a full recheck of affected pages.
